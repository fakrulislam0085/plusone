# Physics I - Newton's Laws of Motion

**Original demo material, not official Polito notes.** Based on OpenStax University Physics Volume 1, Chapter 5.

## Objectives
- LO1: Distinguish velocity from acceleration and apply Newton's first law.
- LO2: Compute acceleration from the vector sum of external forces and mass.
- LO3: Identify Newton's third-law force pairs on different interacting bodies.
- LO4: Construct a free-body diagram with only forces acting on the chosen body.
- LO5: Reason about static and kinetic friction without assuming friction has a fixed value.
- LO6: Explain reasoning to peers and apply a principle to a new situation.

## Core ideas

1. **Newton's first law:** If the vector sum of external forces is zero, the acceleration is zero; velocity remains constant, including remaining at rest.
2. **Newton's second law:** ΣF = ma. Always use the net external force, not just one applied force.
3. **Newton's third law:** A-on-B and B-on-A are equal and opposite, on different bodies, so they cannot cancel in one object's free-body diagram.
4. **Free-body diagram:** Isolate one body and draw only external forces acting on it. Do not draw velocity, acceleration or net force as an additional force arrow.
5. **Static friction:** May take values from zero up to a maximum as needed to prevent slipping. A resting box pushed with 12 N may have 12 N of opposing friction.
6. **Incline:** Parallel weight component is mg sin θ; perpendicular component is mg cos θ. Without friction, a = g sin θ down the ramp.

## Worked example

A 2.0 kg cart has 10 N right and 4 N left. Net horizontal force is +6 N, hence acceleration is 3.0 m/s² right.
