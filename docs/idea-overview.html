Learn Physics I by teaching it to someone who's confused.

PlusOne = learning by teaching a confused AI (from Spiegamelo) + the AI inside the study group with the professor in the loop (from PlusOne v1). Built and working: see the README and docs/example-interactions.md.

1 · The problem (one course, one moment)

Physics I, first year, PoliTO. Students pass the plug-in exercises, but classic misconceptions survive all the way to the exam. Two examples: "a moving object needs a force to keep moving", and "in a crash the truck pushes harder on the car".

Students

In a hall of 200+, nobody asks. Group study turns into splitting the exercises or pasting them into ChatGPT, and nobody checks whether they actually understand.

Professor

Finds out where the reasoning breaks only when they grade the exam, when it is too late to fix it in class.

Why AI is needed

A quiz checks answers. Only an AI can read a free-text explanation, act as a believable confused peer, and find the exact concept where the reasoning fails.

2 · The idea

Students learn by teaching Matteo, an AI matricola (first-year student) who is confused on purpose. They can teach him alone or as a study group (Matteo is the "+1").

  • Matteo never gives answers. He asks naive questions and plants real Physics I misconceptions. The group has to agree on how to correct him. Ask him for the answer and he says: "Non lo so, è per questo che chiedo a te!" ("I don't know, that's why I'm asking you!")
  • The Examiner silently scores every explanation against a Physics I concept graph built from the course slides, and updates each student's mastery.
  • "Why am I stuck?" The graph traces prerequisites: "You struggle with Newton's 3rd law because it requires force-as-interaction, and your free-body diagram explanation was shaky (OpenStax §5.1)."
  • The professor stays in the loop. They choose which topic is unlocked after each lecture, and they see an anonymized view of where the class breaks.

Key insightPlusOne's biggest risk is that the group just asks the AI for the answer. Matteo doesn't know the answer, so the students have to reason with each other.

3 · What we kept from each idea and what we cut

From Spiegamelo

  • Learning by teaching a confused AI
  • Examiner + knowledge graph + root cause
  • Anonymized lecturer flags
  • Boundary guardrail in code

From PlusOne

  • Study group of students + 1 AI
  • Professor provides material and approves the topic
  • Professor sees where the class struggles
  • Peer debate: Matteo asks A whether they agree with B

Cut, and why

  • Grouping by grades: labels students (judges ask "who is left out?")
  • "Understands 90%" dashboards: numbers we can't back up
  • Streaks, social, gamification: not about learning
  • Real-time multi-device chat: too risky in 2 hours

6 · 60-second pitch (draft)

In Physics I, you can pass the exercises and still believe the truck pushes harder than the car. ChatGPT gives you answers. It doesn't check whether you understand. PlusOne flips it: you teach Matteo, an AI freshman who is confused on purpose. He never answers. He asks, and he plants the classic misconceptions. Teach him alone or with your study group, and convince him together. Behind him, an examiner maps every explanation onto the course's concept graph and tells you why you're stuck, down to the slide. The professor sees, anonymously, where the class breaks while there is still time to fix it in the next lecture. If you can explain it, you know it.

7 · The judges' three questions

QuestionOur answer
New, or just faster?Learning by teaching usually needs a human peer who is confused in exactly the right way. AI makes that peer available any time, grounded in our course, and it diagnoses the root cause from the graph.
Helps think, or thinks for them?Matteo structurally can't think for them: he never answers, and a code guardrail blocks solutions. The students do all the explaining.
Who benefits, who's left out?It helps shy students who never ask in a 200-seat hall, and it works by text (no voice) in IT or EN. Risks: students without a laptop (it runs in a phone browser), and data (the professor only gets anonymized concept flags). No grade-based grouping, so no labels.

8 · What we build: a working web app, not slides

Build ✔

  • Streamlit app running on the demo laptop. It is a real web app in the browser, with no frontend work.
  • Tab 1 "Teach Matteo": chat + live graph + "Why am I stuck?"
  • Group mode = a "who's speaking" selector (A/B/C) in the same chat
  • Tab 2 "Professor": flags + class heatmap (if time)

Don't build ✘

  • A static mockup: Level 2 requires a working agent
  • A slides-only pitch: same reason
  • Login, a database, a deployed site, a mobile app
  • Real-time multi-phone chat

Safety net

  • Record a 30-second backup video as soon as the core loop works
  • Fallback API key from Reply (OpenAI) if ours fails
  • Every step logged in logs/session.jsonl = testing evidence
